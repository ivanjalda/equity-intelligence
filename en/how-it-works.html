How it works

Last updated: November 14, 2026

Here we explain, without jargon, what Equity Intrinsic does and where its limits are. If something cannot be calculated with real, reliable data, we show it as “Not valued” instead of inventing a number.

The idea in one sentence

We estimate how much a company is worth based on what it earns and what it owns, and compare that with what it costs on the stock market today. If it costs well below what it is worth, it may be an opportunity. If it costs more, it may be expensive.

What we cover

  • Main list: a selection of large, well-known US stocks, with chart, 5 years of accounts, three valuation methods and a reliability seal.
  • Broad market: thousands of US companies with official SEC accounts. Every Sunday we recalculate their valuation and every day we compare their price with it.
  • Indexes and ETFs, commodities and bonds: exchange-traded funds that represent entire markets, gold, oil, Treasury and corporate bonds.
  • Large funds: the portfolios of large investors, based on SEC 13F reports.

We do not yet cover cryptocurrencies or exchanges outside the US. When we do, we will explain it here along with the method we use.

Three ways to calculate what it is worth (intrinsic value)

  1. Discounted cash flow. We calculate how much free cash the company could generate in the future and bring it to today's value (because a dollar tomorrow is worth less than a dollar today). Assumptions: we discount using a company-specific rate (10-year Treasury bond + its beta × 5.5 points; a standard 10% if there are not enough prices to measure it), long-term growth of 2.5%, the company's own growth between 0% and 12%, and the average free cash of the last 3 years (7 years for cyclical companies).
  2. Graham formula. A classic method that combines earnings per share with expected growth and the yield on high-quality bonds.
  3. Historical multiples. It looks at how many times earnings the market paid for this company in the past (the median P/E, bounded between 8 and 30) and applies it to current earnings.

Automatic review. Every business day a robot recalculates the formulas and checks the published data: that the methods add up, that the accounts sum correctly, that the data is not outdated and that there are no absurd figures. If something fails, it flags it on the website.

Three scenarios. On each company's page you also see a bearish value (2 points less growth and 1 point more discount rate, and the company's own low P/E), the base and a bullish one (the opposite; also, if the company's earnings and cash are today clearly below their average of the last 5 years, the bullish case assumes they return to that average). They show how much the value changes if things go worse or better than normal. The verdict is always based on the base scenario.

The intrinsic value you see is the average of the available methods. If one is more than 3 times away from another, we discard the most optimistic. We also show how much the methods agree: if they agree, there is more confidence.

Banks and insurers are valued differently, with two methods: their book value adjusted for the return they earn (ROE) and the Graham number. Investment funds, real estate companies and other financial intermediaries are not valued yet.

Margin of safety and verdict

The margin of safety is how far below the value the price is. The larger it is, the more cushion if we are wrong about something.

BUYThe price is at least 25% below the value, the company's health is not fragile and the data is reliable.
REVIEWIt looks cheap, but we have detected doubtful data. We do not present it as an opportunity until that is resolved.
WAITIt is between its fair value and 25% below it. We give you an entry price (75% of the value) and an estimated review date.
EXPENSIVEIt trades above its calculated value.

Short, medium and long term

  • Short term: it is in the buy zone and its price is also at least 10% below its average of the last 200 sessions.
  • Long term: non-cyclical companies with positive earnings in each of the last 5 years and sustained growth.
  • Medium term: the rest, including cyclical companies.

It is a guideline, not a promise of how long it will take to play out.

Company health

We review 5 years of official accounts (revenue, earnings, cash, debt, equity) and translate them into simple sentences, with the label Solid, Acceptable or Fragile. A cheap but fragile company does not come out as BUY.

Cyclical companies

Those that earn a lot in good times and little in bad times (for example, commodities or construction). So that we are not fooled by an exceptional year, we use the 7-year average.

Reliability seal (High, Medium, Low)

We check whether the data makes sense: that the accounts are recent, that there are at least 5 years of history, that earnings per share match net income, that the P/E is in a normal range and that the discount is not so huge that it looks like an error. The more checks it passes, the higher the seal. With low reliability, the verdict becomes REVIEW.

Bonds, commodities and indexes

  • Bonds: we compare their current yield with that of the last 10 years. If it is in the highest 25% of that period, it comes out BUY. Corporate bond funds are measured by the “credit spread” (the extra they pay over Treasuries).
  • Commodities: we compare their inflation-adjusted price with their entire history. In the lowest 25%, BUY; in the highest 25%, EXPENSIVE.
  • Indexes: (S&P 500, Nasdaq 100, small companies) we value them by adding up the value of the companies we have valued. With a discount of 15% or more, BUY.

Large funds

Large US managers are required to report every quarter what they hold in their portfolio (13F report). We show them next to our verdict on each company. These reports arrive up to 45 days late, include only US stocks and do not reflect short sales.

Signal track record: no tricks

Each verdict is saved on the day it is issued, with its price, and is never edited. We compare it with three benchmarks over the same dates: the S&P 500, the Nasdaq 100 and the total US market. We started recently, so with few measured signals we cannot yet show that the method works better than chance. We show it honestly, good or bad. We do not import track records from other websites because they are not data that can be verified.

Tools

  • Comparator: up to 4 stocks side by side, with the same rules for all.
  • Calculators: discounted cash flow, Graham formula, dividends and compound interest. You enter the numbers; they are mathematical operations, not promises.
  • Value map: the largest valued companies. The size is their approximate market value and the color is our verdict.
  • Upcoming earnings: estimated date (one quarter after the last report). The company confirms the actual one shortly before.
  • Insider purchases: open-market transactions reported to the SEC (Form 4), of more than $50,000. A purchase is a signal, not a guarantee.
  • Large funds: besides each portfolio, we show the biggest bets and the biggest purchases of the quarter.

News

The news column shows only the headline, the name of the outlet and a link to the original story. We do not copy articles. Headlines belong to their outlets and are updated every few hours. The ones in the right column are in English because they come from US outlets. The news is general information: it is not part of our analysis and does not influence the verdicts.

Photos: some news items show the original outlet's photo and others only text, depending on the outlet. Photos belong to their outlets and are loaded from their servers. If you own content and want us to remove it, write to us at contacto@equityintrinsic.com and we will remove it right away.

What “Not valued” means

An asset appears as “Not valued” when we cannot calculate its value with reliable data. The most common reasons: it is a fund, a real estate company or a financial intermediary (they need their own methods), the company does not have enough years of accounts, or we have not been able to confirm its industry. We prefer to say so rather than show a misleading number.

What we do NOT do

  • We do not predict tomorrow's price.
  • We do not promise returns.
  • We do not invent data: if something is missing, we say so.
  • We do not give personalized advice.
  • We do not show live prices: they are from the last close.

Frequently asked questions

Are prices live?

No. They are from the last market close. We always state this in the header and in the quote ticker.

A company comes out BUY, should I buy it?

It is not an order or a personal recommendation. It is a starting point: it means that, according to our calculations, it trades with a wide margin of safety. Before acting, do your own research, think about your situation and the risk, and read the risk warning.

Why does a cheap company not come out BUY?

Because it may have fragile financial health, doubtful data (low reliability) or a margin below 25%. In those cases they come out REVIEW or WAIT, with the explanation.

How do I know whether the method works?

By looking at the Track record. It is still too early to conclude anything, and we say so on the page itself.
