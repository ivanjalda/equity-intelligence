The essentials

  • Intrinsic value is an estimate, not a fact. It depends on assumptions (growth, discount rate…). With other assumptions, other figures would come out. We show several methods precisely so you can see how much they disagree.
  • A stock being “cheap” does not mean it will go up. A stock can keep falling for years, or be cheap for a good reason.
  • We do not know when or whether the price will approach the calculated value. We make no promises about returns or timeframes.
  • Past results do not guarantee future results. Our signal track record is short and we show it as it is, good or bad. With few measured signals, no conclusion can be drawn.

About the data

  • Company accounts may contain errors, delays or later changes. Although we check for some faults (reliability seal), we cannot detect them all.
  • Prices are end-of-day, not real-time.
  • 13F reports of large funds arrive up to 45 days late, include only US stocks and do not show short sales. A large investor holding a stock does not mean they still hold it or that it is good for you.

By type of asset

  • Cyclical or small companies: their earnings swing a lot and the estimates are less reliable.
  • Bonds: their price falls when interest rates rise, and corporate bonds carry default risk.
  • Commodities and futures-based funds (for example, oil or natural gas): they can drift quite far from the actual price of the product.
